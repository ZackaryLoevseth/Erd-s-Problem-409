# Exact inverse-totient tree rooted at the F=68 witness

## Construction

For a certified value $y$ with $F(y)=r$, every solution of

$$
\varphi(x)=y-1
$$

satisfies $T(x)=y$ and hence $F(x)=r+1$.

The repository exhaustively repeats this operation starting from

$$
y=6{,}148{,}888{,}817.
$$

## Why the enumeration is complete

Suppose

$$
x=\prod_i p_i^{a_i}
$$

and $\varphi(x)=m$. Then

$$
m=\prod_i p_i^{a_i-1}(p_i-1).
$$

Therefore every prime divisor $p_i$ of $x$ satisfies $p_i-1\mid m$. Every possible prime divisor is consequently of the form

$$
p=d+1
$$

for a divisor $d\mid m$ such that $d+1$ is prime. For each such prime, only exponents $a$ with

$$
p^{a-1}(p-1)\mid m
$$

are possible. Choosing at most one exponent for each prime and requiring the exact product of the phi-contributions to equal $m$ enumerates every solution and only solutions.

The published tree was generated by a recursive exact-product search. [`../verify_inverse_tree.py`](../verify_inverse_tree.py) independently regenerates it using divisor enumeration and dynamic programming, without SymPy.

## Interpretation of the empty F=72 level

The exact result is:

> None of the ten $F=71$ values in this rooted inverse tree has a direct inverse-totient predecessor.

It does not imply:

- that no other $F=71$ values exist;
- that all $F=71$ values lie in this tree;
- that $F=72$ is impossible;
- that forward search or another basin cannot produce a larger record.

This is a branch-exhaustion certificate, not a global stopping theorem.
